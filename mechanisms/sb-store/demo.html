A level between a floor and a ceiling: it overflows when full and runs short when empty.

About

A rainwater tank, a home battery, a bank account and a stockpile of drinking water have little in common, except that each holds a level with limits. Things go wrong at those limits: more arrives than there's room for, or more is asked for than is there. sb-store holds that behaviour, tested across unrelated subjects, so a domain model doesn't have to get its edges right again. The subject gives a store its meaning; the store gives the subject its behaviour.

Where it helps

  • Something accumulates and can overflow or run out, such as water in a tank or charge in a battery.
  • A supply is met only in full, or refused, such as a payment from an account or a booking at a venue.
  • How long a supply lasts at the current rate.

Where something else fits better

  • Things that expire. A store doesn't know which units are older; best-before dates and first-in-first-out need an ageing stock.
  • Losses over time. Leakage, evaporation and interest are proportional change, composed alongside the store.
  • Sharing between claimants. Taken one at a time, whoever asks first gets what they ask for. When order shouldn't decide, that's allocation.
  • A line or two of arithmetic. If nothing can overflow or run short, a plain number will do.

Getting started

Set it up in a sandbox with tools/setup.mjs <sandbox> sb-store, then use it in model.js. It keeps no clock, so turn rates into amounts first:

let tank = SbStore.create({ capacity: 2000, level: 300 });   // litres

const today = SbStore.step(tank, caughtRain, dailyUse);      // in before out
tank = today.state;
today.spilled;                                               // what happened: keep it, show it

The full contract is in the manifest.

Try it

A level, a step at a time

Press Step and watch the ceiling. Then turn on the comparison: the same numbers, but filling first and taking afterwards, spill what in before out would have used.

Examples

A household's water through an outage

The taps stop on day 1. UK guidance is 3 litres per person per day for 3 days; water companies must provide at least 10 litres per person per day within the first 24 hours. The stockpile's containers are its capacity, so emergency water beyond them can't be stored. Change when supply arrives, and find the days that go short.

A bank account with an overdraft

The floor is the overdraft limit, below zero, and a payment that won't fit is declined whole: a store with shortfall: 'reject'. Try the £600 rent from the start, then the repair.
