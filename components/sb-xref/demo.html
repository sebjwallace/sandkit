Where a thing appears, and what it's linked to.

About

A sandbox earns trust when people can follow its claims back to where they came from. Codes make that possible, but only if people can find them: where else this rule is used, what that claim rests on, which source that came from. A cross-reference answers both at once. Choose a code, and it lists every place on the page the thing appears, and what the model says it's connected to. Click a place to go there, even into a closed section; click a connection to move to it, and walk the chain with Back to return.

Where it helps

  • Checking a claim. From a rule to the claim it rests on, to the source behind that, and back.
  • Seeing a thing's reach. Grouped by view, the places show at a glance whether a rule is in the table, the prose and the diagram, or only one of them.
  • Finding what's tucked away. Places in closed tabs or folded branches are listed too, and going to one opens it.

Where something else fits better

  • Lighting up what's in view. Pointing at a code already does that, through the highlighting practice. A cross-reference is for what isn't in view, or for following on.
  • Explaining one thing. A pill's card says what a code means; the cross-reference says where it is and what it touches.
  • A guided story. To lead people through things in order, use a walkthrough.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-xref/sb-xref.js"></script>
<sb-xref></sb-xref>

<script>
  function showXref(ref) {
    xref.subject = { ref, title: model.find(ref).name, kind: 'Rule' };
    xref.places = placesOf(ref).map((place) => ({ view: viewOf(place), context: place.textContent }));
    xref.links = model.linksOf(ref);
  }
  xref.addEventListener('sb-go', (e) => goTo(e.detail.ref, e.detail.index));
  xref.addEventListener('sb-follow', (e) => showXref(e.detail.ref));
  xref.addEventListener('sb-back', (e) => showXref(e.detail.ref));
</script>

The cross-reference never searches the page itself. Mark the places a thing appears with data-ref, as Sandkit's components already do; the sandbox finds them with placesOf and goes with goTo, both in the locating practice, which has the whole of this wiring.

Making it reveal, not just list

  • Name the views well. "Chapter 2 › Network" says more than a tag name. The grouping is the shape of a thing's reach.
  • Say why a place can't be gone to. "Hidden by your search" keeps the viewer's choice and tells them what it costs.
  • Give the model's links. Relations in the model's own words ("rests on", "affects") turn a list into a chain people can walk.
  • Float it beside the model, in a panel, so going somewhere never hides the list you came from.

Explanation

The town is warned when rain passes 30 mm (), because the river rises within six hours of heavy rain (). That holds only when the ground is already wet ().
