A workshop's sections in one page: who said what and when, the key parts open, the asides tucked away.

About

A workshop is where the people who know a subject work through a model together: they look at the evidence, ask questions, try things and decide. A notebook keeps that as one page, a section at a time, each with its kind, who it came from and when. A week later it reads as the story of how the model came to be trusted, with people's names on every part.

Where it helps

  • Running a workshop. Add a section as each thing comes up: a source to look at, a question, a quick simulation, a decision.
  • Coming back to one later. The key sections are open and marked, the asides tucked away, and every section says who and when.
  • An audit trail. The codes inside each section are counted on its bar, so you can see where the decisions and questions are without opening anything.

Where something else fits better

  • Chapters of a sandbox, read one at a time: tabs.
  • A story told across several views of a model: a walkthrough.
  • A guided path through a page as it stands: a tour.

Getting started

Every direct child is a section. Describe each with attributes; the notebook never changes what's inside. See the manifest for the full contract.

<sb-notebook id="ridge-fire" heading="Ridge fire · afternoon planning">
  <section heading="Will the wind turn before 16:00?" kind="question"
           by="Ana Lima" time="2026-08-14T13:20" relevance="key">
    <p>… <span data-ref="QST-2">QST-2</span> …</p>
  </section>
  <section heading="Radio channels" kind="discussion" relevance="aside">…</section>
</sb-notebook>

Sections can hold anything. Here they hold plain HTML; in a sandbox they'd hold decision cards, sources, charts and small simulations.

Making it reveal, not just record

  • Put names and times on every section. That's what turns a page of notes into evidence.
  • Mark what matters as key, and the rest as aside. Someone arriving later should see the decisions first.
  • Cite codes inside sections. Mark them with data-ref, and each section's bar counts them, so "where did we decide that?" has an answer at a glance.
  • Name the code prefixes. Set prefixes, such as { DEC: 'Decisions' }, and the filters read in words: choosing Decisions opens just the sections holding one.
