A board of discovery: what's known and decided in columns by the part it plays, and the chain behind anything you point at.

About

Discovery moves in iterations, and each one is easier to follow laid out: what was gathered, what was made of it, what's assumed, what's still open, what was decided and what that set going. This board lays out the sandbox's own items in columns, one for each part they play, and joins them only when asked. Point at anything and it lights the chain behind it, back along what led to it and on along what it led to, with each link labelled; everything else fades. At rest the board stays quiet, so it stays readable with twenty things on it.

The board knows nothing about what it holds. It places each child by its data-ref, in the column that lists it, and draws the links the sandbox gives it. Here the items are plain placeholders; in a discovery sandbox they're the sandbox's own records of what it learnt. Sandkit's practices say which components go together for discovery, and how.

Where it helps

  • One iteration at a glance: everything in it, placed by the part it plays rather than by what kind of thing it is.
  • "What does this rest on?" Point at a decision and see the evidence and constraints behind it, and what it brought about.
  • Making room. Fold the columns you've read, such as what everything rests on, down to their codes; the links still reach them.

Where something else fits better

  • Things moving through stages, with limits and drag: a kanban board.
  • Why one thing is there, back to the words of its sources: a source trace.
  • Notes, images and diagrams placed freely on a canvas: a board to pan and zoom.

Getting started

Put the items in as children, each with a data-ref, then give the columns and the links. See the manifest for the full contract.

<sb-discovery-board>
  <div data-ref="SRC-1">…</div>
  <div data-ref="CLM-1">…</div>
</sb-discovery-board>

board.columns = [
  { title: 'Gathered', refs: ['SRC-1'] },
  { title: 'Interpreted', refs: ['CLM-1'] },
];
board.links = [{ from: 'SRC-1', to: 'CLM-1', label: 'supports', tone: 'good' }];

Set direction="down" to stack the columns as bands, and spacing to give links more room between them when a board gets busy. Give a link a tone (good, warn, bad, accent, muted or text) and its line, arrow and label take it. A column with folded: true starts folded; the viewer's own folding is kept when the columns are set again.

Making it reveal, not just arrange

  • Columns by role, not kind. The same kind of thing can sit in different columns, depending on what it's doing in this iteration.
  • Every link answers a question. Label links with what they mean ("supports", "rests on", "brought about"), and leave out any that don't say something.
  • Let tones say it before the words do. What supports in good, what disputes or refutes in bad, what's only assumed in warn, plain bookkeeping muted: a chain reads at a glance, and the labels confirm it.
  • Set it again for each iteration. Step to another and give the board its items, columns and links as they stood then.
  • Route pointing from elsewhere. Prose, an outliner or a trace can light items through highlight, and reveal unfolds the column holding one.
