A question put to a group, every answer hidden until everyone has answered. With an outcome, a prediction.

About

Asking someone to commit to an answer before they see the result changes how they look at it. A poll asks a question, keeps every answer hidden until everyone has given theirs, then shows them all at once, grouped by option, with who chose what and why.

Asked before a model shows what happens, a poll becomes a prediction: say what you expect, then watch it play out. A wrong guess is the most memorable way to learn something, and a surprise in a group shows exactly where people's understanding differs.

Where it helps

  • Predict, then run it. Before a simulation plays, readers say what they expect. One reader on their own works too: the poll then simply asks before it shows.
  • Checking understanding. A quick question at the end of a chapter, with the answer revealed after.
  • Gauging a group. How sure is everyone that a figure is right, which design do they prefer, what do they think will happen. Hidden answers mean nobody's steers anyone else's.
  • Choosing between pictures. Slot a sketch, an image, a chart or a small prototype into each option.

Where something else fits better

  • Recording what was decided, and why. A poll gauges where people stand; a decision card records the outcome.
  • Many questions at once. That's a survey; keep a poll to one question, and use several polls.
  • Open-ended views. If answers can't be options, collect comments instead.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-poll/sb-poll.js"></script>
<sb-poll question="Which shape will demand take this evening?" reasons></sb-poll>

<script>
  poll.options = [{ id: 'flat', label: 'Steady' }, { id: 'peak', label: 'A peak at six' }];
  poll.people = [{ id: 'you', name: 'You' }];
  const answers = {};
  poll.addEventListener('sb-answer', (e) => { answers[e.detail.person] = e.detail; poll.answers = answers; });
  poll.addEventListener('sb-reveal', () => { poll.outcome = model.shape(); poll.revealed = true; });
</script>

The sandbox keeps the answers, and works out the outcome from the model. Leave outcome out for a plain poll.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Ask before showing. A prediction asked after the model has run tests memory, not understanding.
  • Ask why. With reasons, a wrong answer comes with the thinking behind it, which is what's worth looking at.
  • Show why it went that way, right after the reveal: the model's chart or trace, so a surprise turns into understanding.
  • Read the spread, not the middle. When a group answers, the one person who disagrees may know something the rest don't.
