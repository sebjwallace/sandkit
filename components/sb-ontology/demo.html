Kinds of things and how they relate, a few at a time: what each kind inherits, and what the model finds wrong.

About

Many subjects are mostly about what kinds of things exist and how they relate: species and what eats what, the parts of an energy system and what feeds what, claims and the sources they rest on. An ontology says that once, as kinds, the things of each kind, their properties and the relations between them, with the rules those must keep. This component draws it, a few things at a time, and lets people see what belongs to what.

Where it helps

  • Showing what's wrong where it is. The model's problems sit on the things they're about, in its own words.
  • Seeing what belongs to what. A parrotfish is a fish and a grazer at once; pumped storage is a store and a hydro plant. Point at a kind and everything of that kind lights up, inherited or not.
  • Following who relates to whom. Select a thing and its links light up: what it points to in full, what points to it more faintly.
  • Moving between kinds and things. The same ontology shown as kinds ("a grazer grazes algae") or as things ("the parrotfish grazes turf algae").
  • Telling a big subject a part at a time. One ontology behind several small views, each drawing what its paragraph explains.

Where something else fits better

  • A process or a flow over time. Steps one after another read better as a flowchart or a sequence diagram.
  • Things inside things. When each thing sits in exactly one place, a tree is clearer.
  • A network without kinds. If nothing has a kind, a plain graph of nodes and edges is simpler.
  • Records and their fields. A database's tables and references belong in an entity-relationship diagram.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-ontology/sb-ontology.js"></script>
<sb-ontology></sb-ontology>

<script>
  const reef = document.querySelector('sb-ontology');
  reef.data = {
    classes: [{ id: 'Animal' }, { id: 'Fish', is: ['Animal'] }, { id: 'Alga' }],
    relations: [{ id: 'grazes', label: 'grazes', from: 'Animal', to: 'Alga' }],
    individuals: [
      { id: 'parrotfish', label: 'Parrotfish', is: ['Fish'], links: [{ relation: 'grazes', to: 'turf' }] },
      { id: 'turf', label: 'Turf algae', is: ['Alga'] },
    ],
  };
  reef.show = ['parrotfish', 'turf'];   // the few things this view explains
  reef.problems = [{ ref: 'turf', text: 'Cover not surveyed this year' }];   // what the model found
</script>

Classes say what kinds there are, with is naming the kinds they belong to. Relations name the links things can have, from one kind to another. Individuals are the things themselves, each with its kinds and links. Give each view the whole ontology, and show the things it's about. The component draws and never judges: the model decides what's wrong and passes it in as problems, marked on the boxes and listed in words.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Keep each view small. About twenty boxes at most, chosen for what the words beside it explain. A whole ontology at once is a tangle nobody reads.
  • Split, and say why. Several small views, each with a heading and a paragraph, teach more than one big one.
  • Name kinds that cut across each other. Kinds by what something is and by what it does, such as Fish and Grazer, are where an ontology shows what a list can't.
  • Connect the words. Route sb-highlight to highlight, and announce from sb-point, so pointing at a phrase lights the thing, and pointing at the thing lights the phrase.

Viewers can point at a box to preview its links, select a box or link to keep them, drag boxes to tidy the layout, switch between kinds and things, and point at a key under a drawing to light up those links or kinds (click to keep it lit). The full contract is in the manifest.

Examples

Biodiversity on a coral reef

One ontology of a reef's species, zones and surveys: nineteen things and over forty links, too many to read at once. The model has found three problems, marked where they are. Each view below draws a few of them, chosen for what the paragraph explains, from the same ontology.

Corals and the algae inside them

Reef-building corals such as Acropora millepora get most of their food from microalgae living in their tissue. Here Porites lobata hosts two kinds, one of them Durusdinium, which tolerates heat better. Select to see which corals depend on it.

What feeds on coral

Coral has its own predators. The crown-of-thorns starfish can strip a reef during an outbreak, and the redfin butterflyfish picks at polyps. Parrotfish bite coral too, mostly for the algae on it. Select : every link coming in is something feeding on it.

Grazers keep algae in check

Left alone, algae smother coral. Three grazers keep turf algae down, and the leopard coral grouper eats two of them, so fishing it changes the grazing. Nothing here grazes Sargassum: a gap worth asking about.

Kinds that cut across each other

Kinds by what something is and kinds by what it eats overlap. The parrotfish is both a fish and a grazer; the urchin is a grazer and an echinoderm, not a fish. Switch to Things to see who belongs where.

Surveys, and a gap in the record

Each survey covers one zone and is led by one diver. was; has no diver, a gap the model flags.

A regional renewable grid

One ontology of a region's generators, storage, substations and the places they supply, told a part at a time.

Where the power comes from

Every generator depends on the weather. Wind drives two farms, so a still week hits both at once, while rainfall and river flow change over seasons rather than hours. Select to see what depends on it.

How it reaches people

Generators feed substations, and substations supply towns and industry. Dunmore Steelworks draws on the South Substation alone, but the link between the substations lets power move between north and south.

Storing it for later

Select : it charges from the North Substation when power is plentiful and feeds it back when it's needed. is placed in a town rather than a region, which the model flags.

One thing, two kinds

Pumped storage is both a store and a hydro plant, so it inherits from both: it feeds a substation like any generator, and charges from one like any store.
