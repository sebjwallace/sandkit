A stream of things that happened, in the order they happened.

About

A model that runs over time keeps producing occurrences: an order placed, a message received, a cell dividing, a rule firing. Most of them pass too quickly to notice. A log catches them, so anyone exploring the sandbox can see what just happened, in what order, and look closely at any single moment.

Where it helps

  • Following cause and effect. When something surprising happens, the log shows what led up to it.
  • Watching a system's traffic. Events in and out of a processor, messages between services, packets on a network.
  • Narrating a simulation. Orders and deliveries, turns in a game, steps in a reaction, an agent's reasoning.
  • Connecting the moment to the model. Selecting an entry can highlight the part of the sandbox it concerns.

Where something else fits better

  • Comparing a fixed set of records. If order of occurrence doesn't matter, a table is clearer.
  • Showing current state. A log tells you what happened, not how things stand now. Pair it with a view of the state.
  • Very high volumes. Hundreds of entries a second become noise. Summarise them in a chart or counts, and log only the notable ones.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-log/sb-log.js"></script>
<sb-log limit="200"></sb-log>

<script>
  const log = document.querySelector('sb-log');
  log.append({ time: 'Day 1 · 09:00', text: 'Store North ordered 63 from the DC' });
  log.append({ time: 'Day 2 · 15:00', text: 'Store South sold out', tone: 'bad' });
</script>

Each entry needs only text. Add time, a tone (good, warn or bad), fields to show as columns, or a detail to open on click, as your sandbox needs them.

Making it reveal, not just record

  • Use tone sparingly. If everything is coloured, nothing stands out. Keep it for the moments that matter.
  • Keep the text short and specific. One line that says what happened. Put the specifics in fields and the full record in detail.
  • Say why, where you can. "Ordered 63 (stock 37, below 40)" teaches more than "Ordered 63".
  • Connect it. Listen for sb-select and highlight the related part of the sandbox, so the log becomes a way into the model.

The full contract, including every attribute, event and styling hook, is in the manifest.
