Values over time, with where things stand now always marked.

About

A single number says how things are; a line says how they got there and where they're heading. sb-chart draws a model's values over time, with reference lines for the thresholds and targets that give those values meaning: a reorder point, an overdraft limit, a safe level. The latest value of each line is marked, so the eye always finds "now".

Where it helps

  • Seeing patterns that numbers hide. Cycles, drift, sudden drops, the sawtooth of stock being used and restocked.
  • Comparing quantities that move together. Predators and prey, supply and demand, income and spending.
  • Showing when a line is crossed. A reference line makes "too low" or "over budget" visible at a glance.

Where something else fits better

  • Comparing categories. Values that aren't a sequence over time are better as bars or a table.
  • Just the current value. If the history doesn't matter, a single figure or a meter is clearer.
  • Many lines at once. Beyond three or four, lines tangle. Split them into separate charts.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-chart/sb-chart.js"></script>
<sb-chart heading="Stock"></sb-chart>

<script>
  document.querySelector('sb-chart').data = {
    series: [{ label: 'Stock', values: [80, 64, 51, 38, 92] }],
    lines: [{ value: 40, label: 'Reorder', tone: 'warn' }]
  };
</script>

Making it reveal, not just plot

  • Add the lines that give values meaning. A level is just a level until it sits next to a limit or a target.
  • Use tones for what matters. A warning line in amber, a limit in red, everything else calm.
  • Show enough history to see the pattern, and no more. A week of stock shows the sawtooth; a year flattens it.
  • Mark the events that explain the shape, such as a price rise or a new rule, so a sudden turn has its reason beside it.
  • Show where you are. When the sandbox scrubs through history, set current, so the chart and the rest of the sandbox point at the same moment.

Examples

Predators and prey

Foxes eat rabbits; with fewer rabbits, foxes go hungry; with fewer foxes, rabbits recover. The two populations chase each other in cycles, the foxes always a little behind: compare the rabbits' last peak with the foxes'. Point at the chart to read both at any moment, or at a name below to bring its line forward.

A household budget

A month's balance, with rent leaving at the start and wages arriving at the end, both marked. The overdraft limit is the line not to cross; the lowest point comes just before payday. Raise the spending and watch it dip into the overdraft; move through the month to see where things stand on any day. Clicking the chart jumps there too.
