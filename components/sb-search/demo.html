A collection to search, filter, sort and group, as a list, a grid or a table.

About

Many models hold a collection: services on a network, assets on a grid, species in a survey, cases on a ward. Shown all at once, a collection is a wall. Searched, filtered, sorted and grouped, it answers questions: which services are late, and are they all one operator's? Which assets are old, and where are they?

Where it helps

  • Seeing the shape of a collection. Every filter value says how many items it would show, so the spread is visible before anything is chosen.
  • Asking a question of it. Search for words, or a regular expression with .*, and see what matched marked in place.
  • Comparing in groups. Group by any field with a few values, each group counted.
  • Working at length. Full screen keeps the search and filters pinned while the results scroll beneath them.

Where something else fits better

  • A handful of things. Five items need no search. Show them directly.
  • Watching values change step by step. A table that marks what changed shows a model running; this shows a collection to explore.
  • Things over time, or connected things. A timeline or a graph shows when and how; this shows which.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-search/sb-search.js"></script>
<sb-search></sb-search>

<script>
  const search = document.querySelector('sb-search');
  search.items = model.services;   // [{ id: '1A10', from: 'Leeds', operator: 'Northern', delay: 0 }, …]
</script>

That's enough: every field is searched and sortable, and fields with a few repeated values become filters and groups. Say more through fields (labels, formats, which to filter and group by) and card (what the list and grid show). Where the sandbox knows better, hand it functions: a field's value and compare, or a match and filter of its own.

Write presets for the questions people will ask most, and set preset to start on one. Put the sandbox's own content about the chosen item in a child with slot="detail", and fill it on sb-select: it shows in a pane beside the results, or is left free for content that shows itself, such as a dialog.

Making it reveal, not just list

  • Filter and group by what matters. Status, kind, region: fields with a few values the reader cares about. Derive one with value where the model has numbers, such as a capacity band.
  • Tone the exceptions. Give the late, the failing and the worn a tone, and say why in a field too.
  • Keep the card short. A title, a line, and two or three details. The table view has the rest.
  • Connect it. Listen for sb-select and show the item in the rest of the sandbox; set highlight when something else points at it.

The full contract, including every field option, property and event, is in the manifest.
