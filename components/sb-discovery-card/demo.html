One card of domain discovery: a source, a claim, a question, a decision, a run, each kind with its own look.

About

Domain discovery keeps its record as cards. Every source gathered, every claim made from it, every question still open, every decision taken and every part of the model it leads to is a card with a code. Cards are never edited: a later card supersedes or tests an earlier one, so the record shows what was known and decided at every step.

This component draws one card. Each kind has its own icon and tone, so many of them read at a glance, and source material is always paper. The card works nothing out: whether a claim is corroborated or contested, a decision under review or a test met is for the sandbox to decide, as of whichever iteration it's showing. The card shows what it's given. The domain discovery practice says what each kind is for.

Every kind

Three variants

  • Compact, on a board: its icon, code, status and a few words, small enough for twenty side by side.
  • Normal, the default: the whole card, with what it means in strips and what it rests on folded away.
  • Expanded, in a modal or near full screen: the strips on one side and every fold open on the other.

A source is always compact. Shown in full, it's the source material itself, so the sandbox opens it in its own view; the domain discovery practice says how.

Where it helps

  • Boards of discovery, compact cards in columns by their role in an iteration.
  • Opening one thing fully, expanded, with its evidence beside it.
  • Standing in for a card elsewhere: in a trace, in a run's list of tests, in what a decision rests on.
  • Setting something out formally, outside a discovery loop: a problem and its solution in a workbook entry, or the options, trade-off and recommendation behind a choice.

Where something else fits better

  • A decision a group is recording in a workshop: a decision card that can be edited. Discovery cards are never edited.
  • A source's own text, with the passages marked where they are: a source view. A catalogue of every source: a sources list.
  • Why something is there, as one graph back to its sources: a source trace, which can draw its nodes as compact cards.

Getting started

Give it a card. Only id and kind are needed. See the manifest for the full contract.

<sb-discovery-card variant="compact" openable></sb-discovery-card>

card.card = {
  id: 'CLM-1',
  kind: 'claim',
  title: 'Otters gone?',                        // optional: a few words after the code
  status: 'contested',                          // worked out by the sandbox
  badges: [{ text: 'observed', title: 'Basis' }, { text: '+3', tone: 'warn' }],
  text: 'Otters have left the upper Teme.',     // the compact card's words
  strips: [{ tone: 'main', label: 'Claim', text: 'Otters have left the upper Teme.' }],
  folds: [{ label: 'Sources', items: [
    { passage: { source: 'SRC-1', stance: '+', quote: 'spraint at 4 of 18 sites' } },
  ] }],
  created: '2026-03-05T14:20',                  // the card's own record: every card has these three
  by: 'Agent',
  iteration: 1,
  meta: [{ label: 'Prompted by', text: 'INV-2' }], // and anything else worth keeping
};
card.addEventListener('sb-open', (e) => open(e.detail.ref, e.detail.quote));

Fold items are text, a passage, another card (drawn compact), an option weighed, or a slot holding whatever the sandbox puts there.

Making it reveal, not just record

  • Show it as of an iteration. Step back and the sandbox sets each card again as it stood then. Seeing a claim turn from corroborated to contested is the point.
  • Say who made it, and when. Most cards are written by the agent, so each carries when it was created, by whom and in which iteration, as source material carries its own details, and the agent adds whatever else is worth keeping. The byline says where it came from in the world; the footer says who recorded it.
  • Lead with the main strip. The claim, the question, the decision taken: the card should read in one line.
  • Fold the evidence, don't hide it. Every card says what it rests on, and every passage opens its source at the words.
  • Route pointing both ways. Pointing at a passage or a compact card announces its code, so the same thing lights up everywhere it appears.
