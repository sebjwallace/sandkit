Matrices and vectors as blocks of numbers you can edit and point into.

About

Matrices turn up wherever many numbers act together: transformations in geometry, probabilities in a Markov chain, weights in a neural network, a confusion matrix in data science. Written as a block in brackets, the structure shows: which numbers share a row, which a column. sb-matrix shows matrices and vectors that way, inline in a sentence or on their own, lets people edit the entries, and can colour or highlight rows, columns and cells so the numbers point at what they mean.

Where it helps

  • Showing a model's numbers where they're discussed, inline in the explanation rather than in a separate table.
  • Connecting numbers to pictures. Colour a transformation's columns to match the arrows they become.
  • Letting people experiment. Change an entry and watch what follows.

Where something else fits better

  • Large tables of data. Dozens of rows need a scrolling table with headings, not a bracketed block.
  • Symbolic algebra. Entries are numbers. Expressions with letters belong in an equation.

Making it reveal, not just list

  • Colour what corresponds. If a column becomes an arrow, give both the same colour.
  • Highlight the part in use. When a calculation reads one row, point at that row.
  • Put the numbers in the sentence that explains them, so reading and seeing happen together.
