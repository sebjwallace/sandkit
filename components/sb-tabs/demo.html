One part at a time: chapters, views, anything.

About

Understanding builds a step at a time, and a sandbox that shows everything at once asks too much of whoever opens it. sb-tabs lets a sandbox present one part at a time: one chapter, one view of the model, one stage of an argument. Each panel keeps its state while it's hidden, so people can wander between parts and find everything as they left it. Tab sets nest, so a chapter can have tabs of its own.

Where it helps

  • Chapters. Introduce one idea, then the next, as a well-designed game introduces one mechanic at a time.
  • Different views of the same model. The diagram, the rules and the log, side by side in tabs rather than stacked down a long page.
  • Levels of detail. An explorable view, with the algebra behind it in a tab for those who want it.

Where something else fits better

  • Things people need to compare at a glance. Hiding one to see the other makes comparing hard; show both.
  • A handful of words. Tabs around short text are more effort than reading it.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-tabs/sb-tabs.js"></script>

<sb-tabs id="chapters">
  <section heading="1 · The idea">…</section>
  <section heading="2 · Going further">…</section>
</sb-tabs>

With an id, each viewer returns to the tab they last chose.

Making it reveal, not just hide

  • Order tabs as a path. Each chapter should build on the one before, and say what's next.
  • End each chapter with a way on. A "Next chapter" button that calls next() keeps people moving.
  • Keep headings short and specific, so the tab list reads like a table of contents.

Examples

Chapters of a lesson

A short lesson on the water cycle, one chapter at a time. Use the tabs, the arrow keys, or the button at the end of each chapter.

The sun warms seas, lakes and rivers. Some water becomes vapour and rises with warm air, leaving salt and dirt behind.

Higher up, the air is cooler. The vapour condenses on specks of dust into tiny droplets, and the droplets gather into clouds.

Droplets collide and grow until they are too heavy to stay aloft, and fall as rain, snow or hail, back into the seas, lakes and rivers it all began from.
