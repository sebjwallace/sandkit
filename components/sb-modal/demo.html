A window over the page for the one thing that deserves attention now.

About

Some moments in a sandbox should stop the viewer: the model has just done something surprising, or a choice has to be made before anything can start. sb-modal puts that one thing over the page, dims everything else, and waits. When it closes, the viewer is right where they were, and the sandbox knows how they closed it, so it can carry on, replay, or start the scenario they picked.

Where it helps

  • Explaining a moment as it happens. Pause the model when a shelf runs empty or a population crashes, and show why, with the numbers from that moment.
  • A choice before starting, such as a scenario or a starting point, where each answer leads somewhere different.
  • A closer look at one log entry, table row or part of a diagram, without crowding the page.

Where something else fits better

  • Anything to keep beside the model. A modal hides the page; a viewer comparing the explanation with the model needs it in the page, or in a panel they can move.
  • Notes that don't need to interrupt. A callout in the text is found when the reader gets there, without stopping them.
  • Frequent events. A modal every few steps soon becomes something to dismiss without reading. Keep it for the moments that matter.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-modal/sb-modal.js"></script>

<sb-modal heading="The shelf ran empty">
  <p>Demand rose faster than the warehouse could reorder.</p>
  <button slot="actions" data-close="replay">Replay the week</button>
</sb-modal>

modal.show();
modal.addEventListener('sb-close', (e) => e.detail.reason === 'replay' && replay());

Making it reveal, not just interrupt

  • Open it from the model, at the moment something happens, rather than from a button. The explanation then arrives while the question is fresh.
  • Fill it with that moment's numbers, so it explains this run, not runs in general.
  • End with somewhere to go. Actions such as "Replay the week" turn the explanation into another experiment.
