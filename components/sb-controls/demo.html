Play, pause, step and speed for anything that runs over time.

About

Some things are best understood by watching them unfold, and some by taking them one step at a time. sb-controls gives a sandbox both: play at a chosen speed, pause, step once, reset. It keeps the clock itself, telling the sandbox how many steps to take on each tick, so a sandbox never needs its own timer. At high speeds it takes many steps per tick, so a model can race ahead without the page struggling to keep up.

Where it helps

  • Watching a process unfold. Stock flowing through a supply chain, cells living and dying, a machine running its program.
  • Slowing down to understand. Step once at a time through the moment that matters.
  • Racing ahead to see the long run. Turbo speed shows where a system settles, or whether it ever does.

Where something else fits better

  • Models with no notion of time. A calculation that answers at once needs parameters, not playback.
  • Jumping to any moment. These controls move forwards. A scrubber that moves back and forth is a different component.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-controls/sb-controls.js"></script>
<sb-controls></sb-controls>

<script>
  const controls = document.querySelector('sb-controls');
  controls.addEventListener('sb-tick', (e) => {
    for (let i = 0; i < e.detail.steps; i++) model.step();
    render();
  });
  controls.addEventListener('sb-reset', () => { model.reset(); render(); });
</script>

When a model can come to a stop, such as a machine that halts, set controls.running = false in the tick handler once it does.

Making it reveal, not just run

  • Choose speeds that suit the model. Name them in its own terms, such as "1 day per second", rather than abstract multipliers.
  • Show the clock in the label, so people always know how far the model has come.
  • Make Step meaningful. One step should be one thing people can follow: a move, a day, a generation.

The full contract is in the manifest.

Examples

Life that never ends

Conway's Game of Life: each step is a generation. It runs for as long as you let it. Try Turbo, then Step through a single generation.

A sequence that stops

The Collatz sequence from 27: halve even numbers, triple and add one to odd ones. It always seems to reach 1, and when it does, the sandbox stops the clock.
