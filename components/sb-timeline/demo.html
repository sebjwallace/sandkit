Events laid out in time, in lanes, with a line for now and the stretch that matters in view.

About

Many subjects are stories of things happening: a war, an illness, a project, the history of an idea. Told in words, the events come one after another, and what's lost is how they sat against each other: which ran at the same time, what came just before what, how long one thing took next to another. A timeline puts every event in its place in time, in lanes for the people, places or threads involved, so all of that shows at a glance.

Where it helps

  • What overlapped. Spans in separate lanes show what was going on at once, and what had to wait for what.
  • Where things stood at a moment. Set now and everything still to come fades, while spans running through it fill up to it. Let viewers drag it, and the model says what was true then.
  • Pointing the reader at a stretch. Prose about a single year can set view, and the timeline glides there.
  • A running model's history. Add events as the model makes them, and the timeline becomes a record of what happened, and when.

Where something else fits better

  • A measure that changes over time. A chart draws it as a line; a timeline is for events.
  • Moving through bounded progress. A scrubber is the control for that; pair one with a timeline to play history at a steady pace.
  • Who said what to whom. A sequence diagram shows messages between parties, in order.
  • Many things moving through the same stages. A board shows where each one is now.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-timeline/sb-timeline.js"></script>
<sb-timeline scrubbable></sb-timeline>

<script>
  const timeline = document.querySelector('sb-timeline');
  timeline.lanes = [{ id: 'body', label: 'Body' }, { id: 'immune', label: 'Immune system' }];
  timeline.events = model.events;                  // [{ id, label, at, end, lane, note, tone }]
  timeline.format = (day) => `Day ${day}`;
  timeline.now = model.day;

  // Dragging now asks the model; the model decides, and the timeline shows it.
  timeline.addEventListener('sb-now', (e) => { model.goTo(e.detail.value); timeline.now = model.day; });
</script>

Times are plain numbers in the sandbox's own unit, and format says how to show them. An event with an end is a span; without one, it's a moment. Viewers drag to pan and hold Ctrl or ⌘ to zoom, and the timeline keeps their view until the sandbox sets view again.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Choose lanes that make the point. Two sides of a race, cause and effect, plan and reality: whatever should be read side by side.
  • Let now tell the story. Step or drag now forward and say, beside the timeline, what's true at that moment.
  • Glide to the part that matters. Give prose buttons or links that set view, so the reader looks where the words are.
  • Colour the turning points. A tone on the few events that changed everything makes them stand out from the rest.

Examples

Fighting off a virus

Three weeks of a viral infection, from the body's side and the immune system's. Drag the line for now, or play it, and watch the order things happen in: the symptoms aren't the virus itself but the body fighting it, which is why they arrive after the peak and linger after it has passed. Lasting protection comes last of all.
