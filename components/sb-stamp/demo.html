A status pressed onto the page like a rubber stamp, saying where a thing stands and who put it there.

About

Sandboxes are full of things whose standing changes: claims get verified, decisions get signed off, sources arrive and are later replaced. Shown as words or coloured pills, that standing looks like everything else on the page. A stamp is a different material. Ink pressed onto a card reads as an act, by someone, at a time, and stands apart from what it's pressed onto.

Where it helps

  • Trust. Which claims the model's tests verify, which are failing, and which are only assumptions or inferences waiting to be confirmed.
  • Decisions. Approved, rejected, agreed in a workshop, signed off by someone.
  • Material arriving and ageing. A source received on a date, a record superseded by a later one, a code retired.

Where something else fits better

  • A code to point at. An identifying code people hover and click is better as a pill.
  • The decision itself. The options, the reason and the people belong on a decision card; a stamp is the small mark pressed onto it.
  • Many statuses at once. A stamp on every row of a long list is noise. Stamp the few things whose standing matters, and use a table column for the rest.

Choose a shape (rect, seal or date) and a tone (good, warn, bad or neutral); each tone brings its icon, and icon picks another. Put who stamped it in the who slot. When the model moves past it, set stale to say why.

Making it reveal, not just record

  • Set stamps from the model, never by hand. A Verified stamp written once stays saying so after the model has changed. Run the model's tests on load and after every change, and set each stamp from the result.
  • Mark what's gone stale. Stamp the version something was approved at, and when the model moves on, set stale with the reason, so an old sign-off can't pass for a current one.
  • Say who and against what. "Confirmed by Dr Okafor" and "verified against SRC-3" are evidence; "Confirmed" alone is a claim.
  • Pick the icon that's true. Each tone brings one, but a superseded record isn't a warning, it's out of date: icon="clock".

The full contract is in the manifest.
