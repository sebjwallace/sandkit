Screens sketched in plain HTML, to point at and click through.

About

Many subjects have a screen at the front of them: a booking system, a checkout, a control panel, a form someone fills in. A wireframe shows what people see and do there, without the distraction of a finished design. Here it's written in plain HTML, so anything a web page can lay out, a wireframe can too, and it can be one screen or a whole flow to click through.

Where it helps

  • Seeing a system from the user's side. Put the screen beside the model, so a click on the screen moves the model and the model's state shows on the screen.
  • Walking through a journey. Click from screen to screen, and watch what each step sets off behind the scenes.
  • Talking about a design. Prose can point at a button, and the button lights up.

Where something else fits better

  • What happens behind the screen. A sequence diagram or flowchart shows the steps; pair it with the wireframe.
  • The knobs of a model. Plain sliders and controls are clearer than a mock screen when the subject isn't the interface itself.
  • A finished-looking app. This draws sketches on purpose, so people discuss what's there rather than how it looks.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-wireframe/sb-wireframe.js"></script>

<sb-wireframe>
  <section data-screen="basket" heading="Basket">
    <h2>Your basket</h2>
    <div class="sb-wireframe__text"></div>
    <button class="sb-wireframe__primary" data-go="pay" data-ref="checkout">Checkout</button>
  </section>
  <section data-screen="pay" heading="Payment">
    <input placeholder="Card number" data-ref="card">
  </section>
</sb-wireframe>

Each child marked data-screen is a screen, shown one at a time, with a bar naming them all; leave it out for a single screen. data-go moves to another screen when clicked. Mark the parts worth talking about with data-ref, to point at them, highlight them, or hear when they're clicked. Helper classes draw rows, stacks, boxes, image and text placeholders, and the main button.

Give it a look of its own by setting a few colours and a font on the element: --sb-wireframe-paper, -ink, -line, -fill, -accent and -font.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Let the model decide. Where a step depends on the model (a full slot, a missing name), leave off data-go, listen for sb-select, and set screen only when the model agrees.
  • Show the model's state on the screen. The markup is yours: write the latest values into it, so the screen and the model never disagree.
  • Connect it to what happens behind. On sb-navigate and sb-select, move a diagram or log along, so every click shows its consequences.
  • Use placeholders for what doesn't matter, so the parts that do stand out.

The full contract, including every property, method, event and styling hook, is in the manifest.
