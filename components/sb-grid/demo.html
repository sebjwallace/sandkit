A grid of cells coloured by state or value, painted by viewers and changed by the model.

About

Some things happen across space: a fire moving through a forest, a rumour through a town, heat through a pan, life and death in a petri dish. A grid divides the space into cells, each in a state or holding a value, and the model decides how each cell changes from its neighbours. Simple rules, run over many cells, can make surprising patterns, and seeing them unfold is the best way to understand them.

Where it helps

  • Spread and contagion. Fire, disease, gossip, a new idea taking hold.
  • Cellular automata. The Game of Life, sand piles, traffic on a road.
  • Fields of values. Heat, pressure, density or rainfall across an area, as a heat map.
  • Trying things directly. Paint cells to start a fire, add heat or place walls, and watch what follows.

Where something else fits better

  • A few named things in places. A graph or a diagram says more than a grid of mostly empty cells.
  • Positions that aren't on a grid. A coordinate plane places things anywhere.
  • The totals over time. Plot the legend's counts on a chart to see the whole story.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-grid/sb-grid.js"></script>
<sb-grid paintable></sb-grid>

<script>
  const grid = document.querySelector('sb-grid');
  grid.states = { 0: { label: 'Empty', tone: 'surface' }, 1: { label: 'Tree', tone: 'good' }, 2: { label: 'Fire', tone: 'bad' } };
  grid.cells = model.forest;                         // rows of values

  // Painting asks the model; the model changes, and the grid shows it.
  grid.addEventListener('sb-paint', (e) => { model.ignite(e.detail.row, e.detail.column); grid.cells = model.forest; });
</script>

Give states for cells that are one of a few kinds, or a scale for numbers, shown as a heat map. The legend counts each state as the model runs. With paintable, dragging across cells fires sb-paint for each one; the grid never changes itself, so the model stays in charge.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Let people poke it. Painting a spark or a wall and watching the response teaches more than any number of runs.
  • Point at a state in the legend, or with highlight, to see where all of its cells are at once.
  • Mark what changed with mark-changes when few cells change at a time, so each step's effect stands out.
  • Pair the counts with a chart, so the pattern in space and the story over time sit side by side.

Examples

A forest fire

Click or drag across the trees to start a fire. Each step, burning trees burn out and may set their neighbours alight; the drier the forest, the more likely. Point at the fire or the trees still standing to see where they are, or at the legend.

Heat spreading through a plate

A metal plate whose edges are kept cold. Drag across it to place heaters, and watch the heat spread until it settles. Each cell becomes the average of itself and its neighbours; outlined cells are still changing, and as the plate settles they disappear. Look along the middle row: however you place the heaters, it smooths into a gentle slope.

The Game of Life

A cellular automaton with two rules. A live cell with two or three live neighbours survives; a dead cell with exactly three comes to life; everything else dies or stays empty. Draw cells, or drop in a glider, and press Play: outlined cells are the ones just born or just died. Point at the living cells to see them all.
