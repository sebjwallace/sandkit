A board to pan and zoom, holding notes, sources, images and diagrams where they belong together.

About

Some understanding comes from arrangement. Put a gauge reading next to the interview that contradicts it, gather every idea about one route in a corner, and the board itself starts saying something. sb-board is that space: a wall to pan and zoom across, where the sandbox's own content sits where the agent placed it.

Where it helps

  • Gathering evidence. Sources, readings, photos and open questions side by side, so gaps and contradictions show.
  • Brainstorms. Ideas grouped by theme, where how many sit in each group is itself a finding.
  • Telling a story around a picture. A tour stepping from item to item, with the board gliding to each.

Where something else fits better

  • Things joined by a structure. A network, a flow or a hierarchy is better drawn by a component that knows that structure, laying it out itself.
  • Things moving through stages. A kanban counts and flags them; a board only holds them.
  • Everything at once. A board with a hundred items is a wall nobody reads. Put on it the handful that belong together, and make another board for the next question.

Getting started

<sb-board>
  <article data-ref="SRC-3">…</article>
  <figure data-ref="IMG-1">…</figure>
</sb-board>
<script>
  board.layout = { items: {
    'SRC-3': { x: 0, y: 0, w: 240 },
    'IMG-1': { x: 280, y: 0, w: 320 }
  } };
</script>

Each child names itself with data-ref, and the layout places it by that ref, in pixels at full size. Frames are labelled regions behind the items, grouping them by role: frames: [{ ref, label, x, y, w, h }]. Drag the background to pan; zoom with the buttons, the keys, or Ctrl or ⌘ and scrolling. reveal(ref) glides to a thing.

Marks are the agent's pen: marks: [{ kind, on, label }] with a box, circle, underline or label on a ref, or an arrow from one ref to another. Anchored to refs, they follow things when they move. A mark only comments; anything with content belongs on the board as an item.

Add movable and people can drag things where they think they belong, and an id remembers their moves. changes says what they moved, in words, ready to hand to the agent; once the agent has written them into the layout, they drop away on their own.

Making it reveal, not just hold

  • Let position mean something. Left to right for before and after, near for related, far for unrelated. Say what the arrangement means in words beside the board.
  • Treat a move as something said. Someone dragging a reading next to an account is making a claim about how they connect. Ask them why, and record it.
  • Name the groups. A frame called "Readings" or "Worries" says what its things have in common, and how full it is becomes a finding.
  • Put the contradiction side by side. The reading and the account that disagrees with it, close enough to read together.
  • Glide to what the words are about. Prose that says "the photo from the bridge" can reveal it, so the eye follows the argument.

Examples

Why did the Wenning Bridge flood?

Evidence gathered for a flood inquiry: the gauge says the river peaked late, an interview says the water came early. Readings and accounts in frames either side of the photo, open questions below. The agent's pen marks point out the conflict and the gap. Drag things to where you think they belong, and the marks follow; the board says what you moved, in words the agent can take in.
