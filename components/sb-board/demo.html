Things moving through stages, as cards in columns, with the bottlenecks counted and every move marked.

About

Plenty of things move through the same steps, one after another: orders through a warehouse, patients through a hospital, bills through parliament, applications through an office. Each thing on its own is easy to follow. What's hard to see is the whole flow: where things are piling up, which ones are stuck, and what one slow step does to everything behind it. A board lays the stages side by side, so you can see all of that at once.

Where it helps

  • Queues and bottlenecks. Give a column a limit and watch it go over as work arrives faster than it leaves.
  • Processes with rules. Let people drag cards and have the model refuse the moves its rules don't allow, saying why.
  • Following one thing. A card that just moved says where it came from, so you can follow it through the flow.
  • Keeping track of the sandbox itself. A board of questions to explore and ideas to try, moved along as the sandbox grows, keeps everyone working on it pointed the same way.

Where something else fits better

  • One thing through a branching process. A flowchart shows the paths and the step it's on.
  • How queues change over time. Plot the column counts on a chart, next to the board.
  • Hundreds or thousands of things. Cards stop being readable; count them in a table or bars instead.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-board/sb-board.js"></script>
<sb-board movable></sb-board>

<script>
  const board = document.querySelector('sb-board');
  board.columns = [{ id: 'todo', label: 'To do' }, { id: 'doing', label: 'Doing', limit: 2 }, { id: 'done', label: 'Done' }];
  board.items = model.tasks;                      // [{ id, label, column, note, tone }]

  // Dragging asks the model; the model decides, and the board shows it.
  board.addEventListener('sb-move', (e) => { model.move(e.detail.item.id, e.detail.to); board.items = model.tasks; });
</script>

Set items again after every step of the model, and the board marks each card that moved, saying where it came from. With movable, dragging a card or pressing the arrow keys fires sb-move. The board never moves a card itself, so the model stays in charge.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Give the slow stage a limit. A column flagged over its limit says "bottleneck" before anyone has to count.
  • Let the model say no. A refused move that explains itself teaches the rules better than a list of them.
  • Colour the stuck cards. Put how long a card has waited in its note, and give it a tone once that's too long.
  • Step slowly. Each step marks what moved, so take one at a time and watch where things stop moving.

Examples

A warehouse at full stretch

Orders arrive, get picked from the shelves, are packed and ship. Pickers can handle three orders a step, but the packers can only handle as many as there are packers. Step through it and watch the packing column: with two packers it slowly falls behind, and the orders waiting there turn amber, then red. Add a third packer and see how quickly the queue drains. Click an order to follow it.

How a bill becomes law

Drag a bill, or focus one and press the arrow keys, to move it through parliament. Each bill moves forward one stage at a time, and can always be withdrawn back to the start. The committee can only examine two bills at once, so it's where bills stall: every other bill that moves past one stuck there makes it look more stuck. Try moving the School Meals Bill into committee, or getting the Data Privacy Bill into law.
