Text with the parts that matter marked, and a note on each.

About

Some of what a sandbox needs to understand comes as text: a call transcript, an interview, a contract, a policy, a witness statement. Most of it is ordinary, and a few phrases carry the weight. This component marks those phrases, like a highlighter pen, and gives each a note: point at a mark and the note appears just above it. The text stays whole and readable, with the reasoning a hover away.

Where it helps

  • Reading the evidence closely. Mark the sentences a model was built from, with a note on what each tells it.
  • Seeing patterns in text. Kinds colour the marks, and the legend counts them; point at a kind to see where it falls, such as every risk clustered at the end of a call.
  • Connecting words to the model. A mark can light up the part of the model it concerns, and prose elsewhere can light up the mark.

Where something else fits better

  • A note that everyone must read. Put it in the open, as a callout, not behind a hover.
  • A stream of events. If the text is really a list of things that happened, a log shows it better.
  • Code. A code view understands lines and steps; use it for programs, not this.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-annotate/sb-annotate.js"></script>

<sb-annotate>
  <p>I've called <mark data-kind="risk" data-ref="contacts"
    data-note="Third contact in ten days.">three times</mark>.</p>
</sb-annotate>

<script>
  document.querySelector('sb-annotate').kinds = { risk: { label: 'Risk', tone: 'bad' } };
</script>

Write the text as ordinary HTML and wrap each phrase that matters in <mark>. data-note is its note, data-kind its kind, and data-ref a name to point at it by. kinds names and colours the kinds, and adds the legend. Clicking a mark pins its note open and fires sb-select, so the sandbox can show what the mark is about.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Say why, not what. A note that repeats the phrase adds nothing; one that says what it means for the model ("past the escalation threshold") teaches.
  • Choose kinds that sort the evidence, such as risk, commitment and good practice, so the legend's counts and fading show its shape at a glance.
  • Tie marks to the model. On sb-select or sb-point, show the part of the model the phrase drives, so reading and the model move together.
  • Mark sparingly. If everything is marked, nothing is.

A support call, read for what went wrong

A transcript marked by kind. Point at a kind in the legend to see where it falls, or at the repeat contacts and the promises made. Click a mark to pin its note and see the case it updates.

Agent Thanks for calling, you're through to Sam. How can I help?

Customer It's about my broadband again. This is the third time I've rung and it still drops every evening.

Agent I'm sorry about that. I can see your last two calls here, and the line test from Tuesday.

Customer Last time they said an engineer would call me back. Nobody did.

Agent That shouldn't have happened. I'll book the engineer myself now, for Thursday morning, and I'll ring you on Friday to check it's fixed.

Customer Fine. If it's not sorted by the weekend I'm switching provider.

A tenancy clause, read for who must do what

The same component on a contract, with obligations, rights and deadlines told apart. Point at every notice period or the deposit.

Clause 7 · Ending the tenancy

7.1 Either party may end this tenancy after the fixed term by giving not less than one month's written notice, expiring on the day before a rent day.

7.2 The landlord may not end the tenancy during the fixed term except on a ground set out in law, and must give not less than two months' notice.

7.3 On leaving, the tenant must return the property in the condition recorded at the start, fair wear and tear excepted.

7.4 The landlord must return the deposit within ten days of the parties agreeing any deductions.
