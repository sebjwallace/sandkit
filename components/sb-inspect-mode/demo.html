Point at anything and be told exactly what it is.

About

A sandbox is full of short labels that stand for something precise: a code, a column, a branch, a value. People guess what they mean, and a guess is where misreadings start. Inspect mode removes the guess. Choose Inspect at the top, point at a label, and a card at the side says what the thing is, in whatever way explains it best. Click to hold the card while you read it.

Where it helps

  • Codes and jargon, such as the groups of a weather report or the codes of a rulebook, where each label needs decoding.
  • The parts of a machine or a system, such as an engine: what each part is, and what it's doing at this moment of the model.
  • Labels in diagrams, where there's no room for more than a word: a question in a decision tree, a box in an architecture.
  • The data behind a thing, with the lines that matter picked out, when that's the clearest answer.

Where something else fits better

  • How do we know this? That's trace mode, which follows a thing back to its sources. Inspecting says what a thing is.
  • Seeing how things connect while you work. That's highlighting, in the sandbox as it normally is. A mode is for a while, then you go back to exploring.
  • Something everyone must read. Put it on the page, not behind a mode.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-inspect-mode/sb-inspect-mode.js"></script>
<sb-shell>
  <sb-inspect-mode slot="modes"></sb-inspect-mode>
</sb-shell>
<g data-ref="piston">…</g>

inspect.explain = (ref) => ref === 'piston'
  ? { kind: 'Part', title: 'Piston', body: '<p>A short plug that slides in the cylinder…</p>' }
  : null;

Anything marked data-ref that the sandbox can explain is outlined while inspecting, including the parts components draw. Everything else is left alone.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Lead with what it means, in plain words, before any detail.
  • Say how it stands now, from the model: the reading, the value, whether it's in force and why.
  • Say what it touches: what it feeds, what decides it, how many cases take it.
  • Choose per kind. A definition suits a term, what it's doing now suits a moving part, a rule suits an outcome. Keep each short enough to read without scrolling.

The full contract is in the manifest.

Examples

Inside an engine

One cylinder of a four-stroke petrol engine, cut away. Choose Inspect and point at any part to see what it is and what it's doing now. Back in Explore, turn the crank and inspect again: the same part is doing something else.

    Two turns of the crank make one cycle of four strokes, and only one of them, power, pushes the engine round.

    Reading an airfield's weather report

    Pilots get the weather as a line of code. Choose Inspect and point at each group to have it read out; click one to hold it.
