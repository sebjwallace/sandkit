Vectors, shapes and transformations you can watch and drag.

About

Much of mathematics is geometry in disguise. A vector is an arrow; adding vectors is walking one arrow then another; a matrix is a way of moving the whole of space. sb-plane makes that geometry something to handle: drag vectors and see their sums follow, then apply a matrix and watch the grid itself bend, stretch and turn, smoothly, from where it started to where the matrix sends it.

Where it helps

  • Vectors and their combinations. Sums, scaling, linear combinations and span, with arrows people can drag.
  • Transformations. Rotations, shears, stretches and reflections, seen as space moving rather than numbers in a grid.
  • Determinants and inverses. Watch the unit square's area change, or collapse to a line when there's no way back.
  • Anything plotted in two dimensions, such as forces, velocities or positions in a physics sandbox.

Where something else fits better

  • Values over time. A chart shows a history better than points on a plane.
  • Three dimensions. This plane is flat. A 3D space will be its own component.
  • Curves and functions. This version draws vectors, points and polygons, not plotted functions.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-plane/sb-plane.js"></script>
<sb-plane basis snap="1"></sb-plane>

<script>
  const plane = document.querySelector('sb-plane');
  plane.objects = [{ type: 'vector', id: 'v', x: 2, y: 1, label: 'v', draggable: true }];
  plane.matrix = [[0, -1], [1, 0]];   // a quarter turn
  plane.progress = 1;
</script>

Making it reveal, not just plot

  • Animate transformations. Jumping straight to the result hides the idea; watching space move shows it.
  • Show î and ĵ. Where the basis vectors land is the whole matrix, column by column.
  • Let people drag. A vector they can move is one they can ask questions of.

Examples

Adding vectors

Drag the tips of v and w. Their sum walks v, then w, tip to tail.

Watching a transformation

Choose a matrix, then slide or play it. The grid, î, ĵ and the unit square all move with it; the square's area is the determinant.
