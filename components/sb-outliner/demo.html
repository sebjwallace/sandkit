Everything on the page you can point at, in an outline: point to light up what's connected, pin to keep it lit.

About

Pointing at something to see what it's connected to is quick, but the light goes out the moment you move to change anything. The outliner lists everything on the page that can be pointed at, each opening into its parts. Point at an entry and everything connected to it lights up, wherever it is on the page. Pin it, and it stays lit while you work, following the model as it changes. Two pins, a fill and an outline, let you keep two things lit at once and compare them.

Where it helps

  • Following cause and effect, both ways: what does this input affect, and why does that output say what it says?
  • Keeping an eye on something while you change the model, such as a town's supply while you switch things off.
  • Comparing two cases, one filled and one outlined, to see where their paths part.
  • A page with many views of one model, where the same thing appears in a table, a diagram and the prose.

Where something else fits better

  • Things inside things, such as a file system, to browse: a tree.
  • Where a thing appears on the page, to go there: a cross-reference list.
  • A page with a handful of things, where pointing at them directly is enough.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-outliner/sb-outliner.js"></script>
<sb-shell>
  <sb-outliner slot="panels" id="highlights"></sb-outliner>
</sb-shell>

outliner.items = [
  { heading: 'Towns' },
  { ref: 'brook', label: 'Brook', parts: [{ ref: 'brook.demand', label: 'Demand: 30 MW' }] },
];
outliner.trace = (ref) => model.traceOf(ref);   // what a ref lights, as refs

document.addEventListener('sb-pins', (e) => light(e.detail.fill, e.detail.outline));

In the shell's panels slot, the outliner gets a button in the header, named by its heading (Highlights by default), that opens and closes it. It floats over the page, dragged by its bar. It never lights the page itself: pointing announces the trace through sb-highlight, pins are sent as sb-pins, and the sandbox lights its own parts, as the highlighting practice describes. Set items again whenever the model changes: pins follow. With Glow on, pinned highlights fade slowly down and back; mix pinned colours with var(--sb-glow) and they fade with them.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Trace both ways. An input should light what it feeds; an output what decided it.
  • Labels with values, such as "Demand: 30 MW", so the outline is worth reading on its own.
  • Say what's off. A town without power, or a step left out, should say so in its label.
  • Keep it short. List the things people reason about, not every element on the page.

The full contract is in the manifest.

Examples

Keeping the lights on

Two plants feed four towns through two substations, joined by a tie. In The grid, opened from the header and floating at the right, pin a town and switch the wind off, then open the tie: its supply path follows, until there's nothing left to light. Switch on Glow to keep the pins in the corner of your eye.

A loaf of bread

Point at an ingredient, a step or what you get, here or in The loaf, opened from the header and floating at the bottom right. Pin the flavour, then leave the dough in the fridge overnight: the new step joins its trace.
