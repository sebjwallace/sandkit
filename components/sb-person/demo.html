Who took part, as a badge you recognise wherever it appears.

About

Sandboxes are full of people: who wrote a source, who owns a rule, who is on call, who answered a question, who takes part in a process. A badge makes each of them recognisable at a glance, the same everywhere on the page, and an AI agent taking part looks different from the people around it.

Where it helps

  • Names on things. Who owns a part, wrote a document, is responsible for a site or assigned to a task.
  • The roles in a process. The dispatcher, the driver and the depot manager, shown beside the steps each one takes.
  • Who did what. Beside a change, an answer or a decision, so the record says who.
  • Following one person. Point at a badge and every badge for that person lights up, through a long page.

Where something else fits better

  • Many people at once. Fifty names in badges is a wall; a table of people, or counts, reads better.
  • Crowds in the model, such as passengers or patients by the thousand. Show them as numbers, a grid or a chart.

Keep one list of the sandbox's people and draw every badge from it, so each person has one ref wherever they appear.

Making it reveal, not just display

  • Always give a ref, so pointing at someone shows everything they're connected to.
  • Mark the agent, so it's clear what came from people and what from an AI.
  • Make missing people visible too. Where something needs an owner and has none, say so in its place, rather than leaving a gap.
