Where things come from and what they lead to: columns of things, with every link between them drawn and labelled.

About

Plenty of things rest on other things. A figure on a report comes from a calculation, which comes from readings, which come from a meter. A test checks a design, which answers a requirement. When something looks wrong, or is about to change, the first question is usually "where did this come from, and what else does it reach?" This component lays the things out in columns, in the order things flow, and draws every link between them, labelled with what it means. Point at anything and everything not linked to it fades, so its neighbours stand out without the rest disappearing.

It knows nothing about what it holds. It places each child by its data-ref, in the column that lists it, and draws the links the sandbox gives it. Here the items are plain placeholders; in a sandbox they're its own cards, rows or badges.

Where it helps

  • "Where did this number come from?" Follow a figure back through what calculated it to the readings and the meter behind it.
  • "What would this change reach?" Point at a requirement, a source or a component, and see what rests on it.
  • Spotting gaps. A requirement no test checks, or a figure resting on an estimate, shows in the shape of the links.
  • Making room. Fold the columns you've read down to their codes; the links still reach them. Zoom out to see the whole shape, and in to read.

Where something else fits better

  • A process, with steps in order and a way through it. A flowchart shows where a case is and the way it came.
  • A free-form network or state machine, where nothing flows one way. A graph suits it better than columns.
  • Hundreds of things. Show a few dozen at most, chosen around what's being asked about; a crowded lineage reads like a tangle.

Getting started

<script src="components/sb-lineage/sb-lineage.js"></script>
<sb-lineage>
  <div data-ref="M-1">Meter at Hollin substation</div>
  <div data-ref="R-1">Half-hourly readings</div>
</sb-lineage>

<script>
  const lineage = document.querySelector('sb-lineage');
  lineage.columns = [{ title: 'Meters', refs: ['M-1'] }, { title: 'Readings', refs: ['R-1'] }];
  lineage.links = [{ from: 'M-1', to: 'R-1', label: 'records', tone: 'muted' }];
</script>

Every link is drawn by default. With links-shown="chain", links show only along the chain pointed at, everything leading to it and everything it leads to, which keeps a busy lineage quiet at rest.

Give it a subject and it shows only that thing's lineage: everything leading to it and everything it leads to, as far as back and on allow. Viewers double-click an item to follow it, with a trail back. Viewers pan by dragging and zoom with the buttons at the bottom right, or Ctrl-scroll (⌘ on a Mac); the next button switches that off, to scroll sideways instead, and the last goes full screen. Listen for sb-select to show a chosen item in full, and for sb-follow if you'd rather open its lineage somewhere else, such as a window of its own. The manifest has the full contract.

Making it reveal

  • Order the columns the way things flow, from what everything rests on to what's built on it, so a link going back stands out.
  • Label links with what they mean, and give them tones: what supports in good, what contradicts or fails in bad, bookkeeping muted. A lineage then reads before its labels do.
  • Choose around a question. Make the figure or decision being asked about the subject, so the lineage shows what it rests on and what rests on it, not everything the model knows.
  • Point from the prose. Set highlight from words in the text, so a sentence can show what it's talking about.
