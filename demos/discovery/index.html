Discovering how the board decides

How Ashford International's departure board decides what to show, discovered one iteration at a time. Material is gathered from the world and read into cards: what we know, where we're going, and the model we're building, as real code tested against scenarios and rules drawn from the same material. No card is ever edited, and every status and link is worked out from the cards and the model.

Below the boards, the prototype runs the model on the afternoon's trains, and a mock-up imagines how the model might grow over many versions. For how it all works, look at the practices.

How it connects

One iteration of the loop, on three boards, each laid out by the part a card plays. What we know: the investigations that gathered sources, the values captured from them, what was interpreted, reasoned and assumed, what is still open, and what is still being looked into. Where we’re going: what was in force, what was decided, and what that brought about. What we’re building: entities and domain primitives proposed for the model, integrated into it, or rejected, and the scenarios and rules that test it, passing or failing as they run against the model; each is founded on cards from the other boards, and what an integrated part is comes from its class in the model’s code. No card is ever edited: later cards test or supersede it, and what follows is worked out for each iteration. Point at a card to see what it rests on and what rests on it; click to open it. Click a column’s title to fold it down to its codes.

Versions of the model

Everything in this section is made up, to show how versions of the model and their compression signal might look over many iterations. Nothing here is derived from the model.

Compression = 100 × tests met per decision × arrival × spread. Arrival is 0.5 + 0.5 × the share of new tests met with no change to the model; spread is 1 − the busiest method’s share of all decisions. So structure raises it, a patch lowers it, and crowding code into one method costs.
