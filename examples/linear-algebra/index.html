Goal: see linear algebra rather than memorise it: vectors as arrows, and matrices as ways of moving the whole of space.

Chapter 1 · Vectors

A vector is an arrow that starts at the origin. It is also a pair of numbers, and the two are the same thing: the numbers say how to reach the arrow's tip. (2, 1) means two steps right and one step up.

Adding two vectors means walking along one, then along the other. In numbers, add the first parts together and the second parts together.

Try: drag the tips of v and w. Watch where v + w lands, and check it against the numbers. Can you make the sum land back on the origin?

Chapter 2 · Scaling, combinations and span

Multiplying a vector by a number stretches it. 2·v is twice as long; ½·v is half; −1·v points the opposite way. The number is called a scalar, because it scales.

Scale two vectors, then add them, and you have a linear combination: a·v + b·w. Every choice of a and b reaches some point. All the points you can reach this way are the span of v and w.

Try: slide a and b until the tip lands on the red point P. Then show the span, and drag w until it lines up with v. What happens to everywhere you can reach?
