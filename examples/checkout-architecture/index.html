Chapter 2 · Placing an order

The architecture says who can call whom. It doesn’t say what happens when someone actually pays. A sequence diagram does: the parts that take part stand side by side, and each arrow is one message, in the order they happen. Every message travels along one of the architecture’s lines, so as the order goes through, the architecture beside it follows from level to level.

Try: step through the checkout. When is the money taken, and what has already happened by then? Point at a name or a code to see it in both diagrams: a line’s code lights every message that travels along it, there and back.

Chapter 3 · What’s sent

Each arrow in chapter 2 carries something. Inside the shop’s API, parts call each other directly; the four messages that cross a network carry their data as JSON. Follow one order through them: what the Storefront knows, what Orders adds, what the provider sends back, and what the shopper is finally told.

Try: step through the four messages. Where does the order number first appear, and where does it go? Click any line of the JSON to see its path; the ringed lines have a note.

Chapter 4 · Where it’s kept

Most messages leave nothing behind. A few write to the shop database, and what they write is all the shop remembers once the shopper has gone: how many mugs are left, what was ordered, and whether it was paid for. The diagram shows the tables and how they refer to each other; beside it are their rows, with the cells each write changes marked.

Try: step through the three messages that write. After message 6, the order exists but isn’t paid for. What would the database say if the card were declined then? Chapter 5 finds out.

Chapter 5 · When payment fails

So far every card has gone through. When one doesn’t, the shop has already done things it now has to undo: two mugs are held back for an order nobody will pay for, and the order sits in the database as pending. The conversation is the same as chapter 2’s up to the charge; from there it goes another way.

Try: step through from the charge. What gets undone, and what’s kept? Why isn’t the order simply deleted? Then read the last step, about a reply that never arrives.
