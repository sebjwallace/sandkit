# How low inertia changes the first seconds

An analysis by Claude, 24 September 2026, of the control room interview and the August fault log.

## What the fault log shows

Of the six trips in August, the three on days of strong wind reached their lowest frequency in 3 to 4 seconds; the three on days of light wind took 6.5 to 8 seconds. The strong-wind trips also fell lower for their size.

## What the interview adds

The engineer put this down to less inertia: with more wind and solar there is less spinning mass, so the drop is faster.

## What follows

Falls are faster on windy nights, roughly twice as fast in this small sample. The first two seconds, before fast response arrives, matter most on those nights.

This rests on six trips in one month. It is a reading of the evidence, not a measurement of inertia.
